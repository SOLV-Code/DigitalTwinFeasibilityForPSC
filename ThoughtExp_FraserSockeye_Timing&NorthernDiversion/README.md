# Fraser Sockeye Timing and Northern Diversion


## Context

A variable proportion of returning adult Fraser River sockeye migrate along a northern route around Vancouver Island through Johnstone Strait, while the remainder migrates along a southern route along the Westcoast of Vancouver Island through Juan de Fuca Strait. The northern diversion rate affects catchability in different fisheries and the portion of the return migrating through U.S. fisheries, but also affects the interpretation of in-season assessment surveys, such as test fisheries. Both run timing and diversion rate are linked to marine conditions and clear differences between individual Fraser sockeye stocks have been observed. 

Models developed a decade ago evaluated many environmental covariates (e.g., surface currents, wind stress), and tested thousands of statistical relationships between environmental variables and fisheries data. 

At the time. the analyses focused on annual pre-season forecasts of aggregate diversion rate for the whole season across all Fraser sockeye stocks, but daily diversion rate varies substantially throughout each season, and even the underlying patterns are highly variable.

A digital twin approach could be used to update the existing models and explore the potential for real-time in-season updates and short-term in-season forecasts, given that much more environmental data is now available much quicker.

[McKinnell (1997)](https://github.com/SOLV-Code/DigitalTwinFeasibilityForPSC/tree/main/AnnotatedBibliography#mckinnell-1997---fraser-sockeye-diversion-rate) systematically reviewed previous estimates of diversion rate and explored biases associated with simplifying assumptions related to timing and abundance moving through different fisheries. Pre-season forecasting of diversion rates was not attempted at the time, because diversion rate estimates were mainly used for post-season evaluation of Canada-U.S. harvest sharing targets.

As fisheries and the bilateral management approach evolved, estimates of timing and diversion became more important for pre-season planning. A comprehensive analysis of candidate forecasting models was completed in 2016 ([DFO (2016)]( https://github.com/SOLV-Code/DigitalTwinFeasibilityForPSC/tree/main/AnnotatedBibliography#dfo-2016---fraser-sockeye-timing-and-diversion), [Folkes et al. 2018](https://github.com/SOLV-Code/DigitalTwinFeasibilityForPSC/tree/main/AnnotatedBibliography#folkes-et-al-2018---fraser-sockeye-timing-and-diversion)). Environmental variables included sea surface temperature and current velocity at different locations and times (e.g., near-real time estimates from North East Pacific Salmon Tracking And Research, NEPSTAR) , Ekman transport (variations in the southward displacement of young fish by equatorward flowing boundary currents), wind stress, sea level (mean along west coast of Vancouver Island, north-south differences along east coast of Vancouver Island), Fraser River discharge, El Niño events, and even variations in the earth's geomagnetic field. ## How are the existing models being used by the PSC?

As a starting point, assuming the process is similar to the pre-season abundance forecasts. Will follow up with Fraser folks to confirm/revise:

* Performance of alternative models is evaluated periodically
* Annual forecasts from top-ranked alternative models are compared in the annual pre-season planning discussions.
* Early in-season observations from test fisheries and commercial fisheries are compared against pre-season forecasts of timing and diversion, and fisheries are adjusted in response.
* As the season progresses, the pre-season forecasts of timing and diversion are set aside and fishery-based in-season estimates are for weekly planning of stock assessment and harvest.
* Weekly in-season information packages for the Fraser River Panel (available [here](https://www.psc.org/publications/fraser-panel-in-season-information/fraser-river-panel-information-package/)) include pre-season forecasts and in-season estimates of diversion rate.
* Annual post-season summary reports by the Fraser River Panel (available [here](https://www.psc.org/publications/annual-reports/fraser-river-panel/)) include a comparison of pre-season, post-season, and daily in-season estimates estimates of diversion rate.
